The financial layer gets messy
before anyone admits it.

When a business grows faster than its financial systems, the founder carries the gap. Everything sits in one person's head because no structure exists to hold it.

You don't have a clear picture of your financial position
Revenue is visible. Profit is a guess. Cash position requires manual work to calculate. When someone asks how the business is doing, the honest answer is you're not entirely sure — because the financial data hasn't been organised.
Reporting takes hours of manual assembly every month
The same report, rebuilt by hand each time. Pulling from multiple sources, reformatting, verifying. Hours spent producing information that should already exist — in a system, updating itself.
Financial workflows have no documented process
AP, AR, reconciliations, expense tracking — done by whoever has bandwidth, however they figure it out. When they leave or are unavailable, nothing continues cleanly. There's no system, just individuals.
The books are behind and investor conversations are coming
Messy or outdated records are manageable until they're not. A funding round, a new hire, or an audit makes the gap very visible very quickly — and cleaning it up under pressure is expensive.

These problems don't solve themselves. They need accurate records, systems built around the financial workflows, and someone accountable for keeping both current — which is exactly where I work.

What exactly is a Financial Operations Assistant?
A Financial Operations Assistant sits between pure bookkeeping and general operations support. A bookkeeper maintains records. A general VA handles tasks. A Financial Operations Assistant builds and maintains the financial systems — the processes, workflows, reporting structures, and controls — that make financial records accurate and useful. I handle the full layer: keeping the books current, producing reporting you can act on, and documenting the workflows so the financial operations run consistently without depending on one person.
Are you looking for employment or offering freelance services?
Both, and I'm clear about the difference. I'm open to permanent full-time remote employment in financial operations, finance systems, or bookkeeping roles, and I'm available for project-based or monthly retainer consulting engagements. The skills are the same either way — the arrangement is different. If you're a recruiter or employer, the About page has my full background and credentials.
How is this different from hiring a bookkeeper or a general VA?
A bookkeeper maintains records. A general VA handles inbox and calendar tasks. I work at the intersection: accurate financial records, plus the reporting systems that make those records useful, plus the documented financial workflows that make the whole thing sustainable. My background in Economics, Mathematics, and Financial Engineering means I understand what numbers imply — not just how to organise them. If you need someone who can take ownership of the financial and operational layer and build infrastructure that holds, that's where I work.
Do you have remote work experience?
Yes. At Pitch Investors I supported a US-based CEO for 10 months remotely — handling financial deliverables, investor communication, and operational coordination. I operate asynchronously by default, communicate proactively, and don't need to be checked on to produce finished work.
What does a typical engagement look like?
We start with a free 30-minute discovery call. If there's a fit, I recommend a project engagement or a retainer depending on your situation. Week one is a financial audit — I review your books, reporting, and workflows, identify the gaps, and confirm priority order. Work starts against agreed scope. Project engagements close with full documentation. Retainers continue monthly.
What does this cost?
Scope and investment are discussed on the discovery call, once I understand what actually needs doing. Project engagements are priced on deliverables and hours — confirmed before work begins. Retainers are based on monthly scope agreed at onboarding, billed on actuals. No surprises on either side.
Do you work with clients outside Kenya?
Yes. I work remotely with clients and employers worldwide across time zones, both synchronously and asynchronously. Most communication happens via Slack or email, with weekly sync calls where needed.
What tools do you work with?
QuickBooks, Xero, Zoho Books, Excel, Google Sheets, Power BI, Notion, Zapier, Make, Airtable, ClickUp, Trello, Asana, Slack, Zoom, and others. If you use something not on this list, I pick it up quickly. The goal is to reduce friction, not add it.
Can you replace my accountant or CFO?
No — I'll be direct about that. I'm not a licensed CPA and don't file statutory accounts or handle tax compliance requiring registration. What I do is the financial operations layer: accurate records, useful reporting, and the documented workflows that make both sustainable. If you have a CFO or accountant, I work alongside them — handling the day-to-day financial operations so they can focus on strategy.
