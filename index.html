Sound familiar?

Most founders are great at their business.
Running the admin around it is a different story.

When a business grows faster than its systems, the founder becomes the system. Everything routes through you because nothing else exists to hold it.

You don't know exactly how the business is doing financially
Revenue is visible. Profit is a guess. Cash position requires work to figure out. When someone asks how the business is going, the honest answer is you're not sure because nobody has organised the data.
Important things keep slipping through the cracks
Not because you're not on top of it, but because the business doesn't have the capacity. Follow ups don't happen. Reconciliations are weeks behind. Things that should have been done last month are still waiting.
You are the only one who knows how things get done
Decisions, approvals, context all come back to you. When you're not available, work stops. Nothing is written down. Every new person needs a full walkthrough you don't have time to give.
You are doing too many jobs at once
Finance, operations, communication, execution. You're carrying more than one person should because handing things off requires structure that doesn't exist yet.

These problems don't go away by working harder. They go away with better structure, clearer financial information, and someone you trust to follow through which is exactly what I provide.

Common questions

How is this different from hiring a regular virtual assistant?
A VA does what you tell them to, when you tell them. I build systems, anticipate needs, and manage operations proactively. My financial analysis background means I understand the business impact of each decision. The difference is a task taker versus a partner who sees the full picture.
Do you have remote work experience?
Yes. At Pitch Investors I supported a CEO for seven months handling coordination, investor communication, and financial deliverables remotely, with the CEO based in the US. I operate both synchronously and asynchronously by default and communicate proactively rather than waiting to be checked on.
What does a typical engagement look like?
We start with a free 30 minute discovery call. If there's a fit, I recommend either a project engagement or a retainer depending on your situation. The first week is a deep dive audit of your books, workflows, and systems. By week two, you're seeing results. Project engagements close with full documentation. Retainers continue monthly.
Do you work with clients outside Kenya?
Yes. I work remotely with clients worldwide and operate both synchronously and asynchronously across time zones. Most communication happens via Slack or email, with scheduled weekly sync calls. The tools I use work regardless of where you're based.
What tools do you work with?
QuickBooks, Xero, Fresh Books, Excel, Power BI, Google Workspace, Notion, Zapier, Airtable, ClickUp, Trello, Asana, Slack, Zoom, and more. If you use something not on this list, I pick it up quickly. The goal is to reduce friction, not add it.
How do you handle confidential financial data?
Confidentiality is standard. I sign NDAs on request, use your preferred password manager, and follow any security protocols you have in place. My background in financial analysis means I'm accustomed to handling sensitive data with discretion.
Are you available full-time or part-time?
Both. Full-time remote employment, part-time retainers, and fractional arrangements all work. For freelance and agency placements, I operate at $15/hr with hours agreed upfront.
Can you replace my accountant or CFO?
No, and I'll be direct about that. I'm not a licensed CPA and don't file statutory accounts or handle tax compliance that requires registration. What I do is the operational and reporting layer: clean books, accurate records, usable dashboards, and financial models. If you have a CFO or accountant, I work alongside them to handle the day to day so they focus on strategy.
