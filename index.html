What is a Finance and Operations Partner?
A Finance and Operations Partner takes ownership of the full operational and financial layer of a business. That means accurate financial records, reporting that surfaces useful information, automated workflows that remove repetitive manual work, documented processes so the business doesn't depend on one person's memory, and direct executive support for the CEO. The role reports directly to the founder not through an operations director or team lead. One person, accountable for the whole layer.
How is this different from hiring a bookkeeper, a coordinator, or a virtual assistant?
A bookkeeper maintains records and stops there. A coordinator manages tasks and stops there. A Finance and Operations Partner works across all three areas, financial operations, systems and automation and executive support and owns the outcomes, not just the tasks. My background in Economics, Mathematics, and Financial Engineering means I understand what the numbers imply, not just how to record them. When I see a discrepancy, I investigate why. When I build a process, I design it to run without me. The difference is ownership versus execution.
Do you have remote work experience?
Yes. At Pitch Investors I supported a US based CEO for 8 months remotely delivering financial packages, managing investor communication and coordinating operations across time zones. I operate asynchronously by default, communicate proactively and produce finished work without needing to be checked on.
What tools do you work with?
QuickBooks, Xero, Zoho Books, Excel, Google Sheets, Power BI, Notion, Zapier, Make, Airtable, ClickUp, Trello, Asana, Slack, Zoom, Google Workspace, and others. If you use something not on this list, I pick it up quickly.
Can you replace my accountant or CFO?
No, I am direct about that. I am not a licensed CPA and do not file statutory accounts or handle tax compliance requiring registration. What I do is the financial operations layer preparing accurate records, useful reporting, documenting workflows and direct executive support. If you have a CFO or accountant, I work alongside them and handle the day to day so they focus on strategy.

Executive Support · [Company Name]

Inbox chaos → eight-category system that runs itself

The Problem

One inbox. Everything mixed: client emails, newsletters, receipts, phishing attempts. Nothing sorted. Nothing prioritized. Nothing caught the phishing email before damage.

What I Built
  • Full inbox audit — every message type identified
  • 8-label system designed (Work, Personal, Newsletters, Subscriptions, Announcements, Important, Not Important, Defer)
  • Gmail filters configured so new mail sorts itself on arrival
  • Written SOP for anyone maintaining the system
The SOP

Step 1: Every incoming email hits the inbox. Gmail filter rules automatically label it based on sender, subject line, or content keywords.

Step 2: Labels are color-coded. Work = blue (urgent). Personal = green. Newsletters = gray (low priority). One glance shows what needs attention.

Step 3: Defer label is for "deal with later" — batch them Thursday morning. Important is for time-sensitive. Phishing attempts get flagged and isolated.

Step 4: Weekly reset. Archive read items. Keep unread in inbox. Takes 5 minutes.

What Changed
Outcome

✓ Inbox stays organized without manual re-triage

✓ Phishing threats caught immediately

✓ Time to find important email: 30 seconds

✓ Anyone can maintain this system

The Problem

What I Built

Same company, three bookkeeping systems — which actually works

The Problem

Question: If you have the same business, same data, same transactions — what's different between Excel, QuickBooks Online, and Xero? Most founders don't know. They pick based on price or what they've heard.

What I Built
  • Set up identical P&L for same 12-month period in all three platforms
  • Recorded the same 48 transactions in Excel, QBO, and Xero
  • Compared: setup time, ongoing maintenance, reporting capability, integration options, cost

The SOP: How to Choose

Excel: You have <2 staff, transactions <50/month, willing to do manual work. Setup: 2 hours. Ongoing: 3-4 hrs/week.

QuickBooks Online: You have staff, transactions 50-200/month, want automation, USA-focused. Setup: 1 day. Ongoing: 2-3 hrs/week. Cost: ~$30/month.

Xero: You're global, need multi-currency, want best reporting, have 50+ transactions/month. Setup: 1 day. Ongoing: 2-3 hrs/week. Cost: ~$20/month.
